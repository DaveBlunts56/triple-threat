# Scoring from the catch

## Film notes

I reviewed sequences from 0:12 through 0:31 and four-second samples at 2:00, 4:00 and 6:00 in three YouTube bagwork compilations, using successive frames to follow the ballhandler and the nearest defender. The clips illustrate ways to create a shot. Their selection favors successful possessions.

- On the right wing the scorer changes direction against the first defender, takes the baseline and finishes beside the rim. The initial dribble moves the defender before the scorer accelerates. [Isolation sequence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MOGEbEN21ck&t=14s)
- The ballhandler works from the perimeter into the lane, with a defender following from the side. The next sequence starts on the wing again. The scorer keeps the dribble alive until there is room to turn toward the basket. [Wing attacks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YxBVZEDvlpA&t=14s)
- One scorer attacks through the middle. In the next possession a perimeter scorer works against close coverage near the right sideline and rises from outside. The defender's position and the scorer's preferred range give similar starting positions different endings. [Contrasting finishes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w0UwBC8pUm8&t=14s)

## Franchise behavior

Players keep the game's existing shot, pass, post-up and dribble-move decisions. When a receiver would otherwise wait or reposition, a player who favors rim attempts can accelerate toward the near side of the paint. A midrange scorer can dribble toward the near elbow against a close defender. A perimeter shooter can gather in space or slide along the arc away from pressure. Defenders can close each route. Called actions that still need the handler, breaks and late-clock strategy retain priority. Each fictional player's own shot diet determines which read they try.
