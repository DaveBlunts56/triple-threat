# Player models overhaul: design

Date: 2026-11-15. Branch: `feat/player-models`.

## 1. What this is, and where it can land

The owner asked for the players to look like a modern basketball game's: real kit with thickness and seams that moves,
better bodies and faces with expressions, many hairstyles and accessories, physically based skin that sweats, high
quality materials, and levels of detail so weaker computers still run it, built and looked at in stages.

It is a large step up, not NBA 2K. Those players are scanned people with about 100,000 triangles a head, hair made of
strands and cloth authored by hand. Here every player is generated by script from MakeHuman (CC0) in Blender, and the
game runs in a browser on a Chromebook. Up close, generated hair and faces will still look made by a game.

## 2. What there is now

- `tools/blender/build_players.py` makes the 16 people of `assets/source/people.json` with MPFB (MakeHuman), on the
  Mixamo skeleton (52 bones), and writes `assets/players.glb` (13 MB). About 15,000 triangles each.
- The jersey and shorts are separate meshes, but each is a single-sided shell cut from the body's "tights" helper and
  pushed out from it. The colour, trim and numbers are painted at load (`src/render/kit.js`) onto one 1024-pixel
  picture per player, which every part of him shares; there are no normal maps.
- `src/render/people.js` joins every part into one mesh with one `MeshStandardMaterial`. Eyes, brows and lashes are
  in the file but hidden. There are no expressions.
- Hair: `short01`, `short02`, `short04`, `afro01` and none. The pack has ten styles; none is a fade, twists, locs or
  cornrows.

## 3. Ground rules

- **The match is unchanged.** Nothing under `src/sim/` changes. The motions (`assets/motion.glb`), foot planting, the
  ball in the hands and every existing test keep working.
- **The skeleton keeps its 52 bones and their names.** New bones (section 5.4, 7.3) are added as children; no motion
  moves them.
- **CC0 only, and nothing downloaded.** Everything comes from the MakeHuman and MPFB packs already in
  `assets/source/packs/`; every new picture (fabric, pores, sweat masks) is generated by our own scripts.
- **No unclothed body is shipped**: skin under the kit, sleeves and shoes is removed, as now.
- **Invented looks only:** no real person's hairstyle is copied, no brand or logo appears.
- **One file:** `npm run single` still makes one HTML file. It will grow (section 9).
- **The build stays reproducible:** `blender-4.5 --background --python tools/blender/build_players.py` makes
  `assets/players.glb` from `people.json` and the packs, with nothing done by hand.

## 4. The stages

Each stage is built, measured and shown before the next: a contact sheet from Blender of three players before and
after (whole body, head, legs), stills in the game at Low and High, the frame rate (section 9), the file sent.

| Stage | What |
|---|---|
| 1 | The kit (section 5) |
| 2 | Body and face (section 6) |
| 3 | Hair and gear (section 7) |
| 4 | Skin and sweat (section 8) |
| 5 | Levels of detail and speed (section 9) |

The build script is split as it grows: `tools/blender/kit.py`, `hair.py`, `gear.py` and `maps.py` (generated pictures)
beside `build_players.py`.

## 5. Stage 1: the kit

### 5.1 The garments

- **Jersey:** sleeveless, cut from the tights surface as now, then given:
  - thickness: 4 mm (Blender's Solidify, outward), with the hem turned up inside by 2 cm;
  - a **collar binding** round the neck opening and **arm-hole bindings**: rings of their own, 1.5 cm wide and 3 mm
    proud of the jersey, as separate pieces of the mesh;
  - **side panels**: a strip down each side from arm hole to hem, 7 cm wide, a region of its own in the picture.
- **Shorts:** knee length, cut as now, then given 4 mm thickness, a **waistband** (a ring 5 cm tall, 3 mm proud), a
  2 cm hem turned up inside, and a **side stripe** down each leg.
- **Drape:** before the kit is weighted to the skeleton, each garment is dropped onto the standing body with Blender's
  cloth simulation (the body as collider, 40 frames, with gravity) so that it hangs and folds as cloth does. The
  folds are kept in the mesh. Its weights are then copied from the nearest skin (Data Transfer), as now.
- **Fit:** looser than now: the jersey 8 cm wider than the chest all round at the hem, the shorts 12 cm at the hem.

### 5.2 The pictures

- **Layout:** the kit picture keeps its two panels (front and back), and gains regions for the bindings, the side
  panels and the waistband, so the game can paint each its own colour.
- **Fabric:** a tiling **mesh knit** (small round holes on a 2 mm grid, with knit ribs) for the jersey and a tiling
  **twill** for the shorts, each a 512-pixel normal map and roughness map generated by `maps.py`, repeated over the
  garment. Shared by all players.
- **Seams and stitching:** lines along each binding edge and the side panels, as a normal map in the garment's own
  picture, generated from the layout.
- **Wrinkles:** the drape's folds are in the geometry; finer creases are a generated wrinkle normal map, stronger at
  the waist and the arm holes.
- **Numbers and lettering** are still painted at load (team colour, trim, number). They also get a raised edge: the
  game paints a height picture with the same ops and turns it into a normal map once per player.

### 5.3 Materials

`MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial` for the kit: roughness about 0.75 for the jersey and 0.85 for the shorts, a faint sheen (as
woven cloth has), the fabric normal blended with the seam and number normals. Both sides drawn, so the inside shows
at the arm holes and the hem.

### 5.4 Movement

Six new bones, with spring motion at run time:

| Bone | Parent | Moves |
|---|---|---|
| `kitJerseyFront`, `kitJerseyBack` | `mixamorig:Spine` | the lower jersey, front and back |
| `kitShortsLeftFront`, `kitShortsLeftBack` | `mixamorig:LeftUpLeg` | the left leg of the shorts |
| `kitShortsRightFront`, `kitShortsRightBack` | `mixamorig:RightUpLeg` | the right leg |

The garment's weights blend from its body bone to its spring bone toward the hem (none above the waist, all of it at
the hem). At run time `src/render/springs.js` turns each spring bone toward where it would hang if it lagged behind
its parent's movement: a damped spring on its angle (stiffness, damping and a 25 degree limit as constants there),
fed by the movement of the player and of the parent bone, run after the motions and before the feet are planted.
Paused, it stops.

## 6. Stage 2: body and face

- **More triangles where they show.** The body keeps 75% of its triangles (now 42%), and all of them on the face,
  ears, hands, shoulders and knees. This is the near level of detail (section 9).
- **Muscle definition:** a normal map per player, baked in Blender from the body before it is simplified, with one
  level of subdivision, onto the game's mesh. 1024 pixels.
- **Eyes:** the pack's `high-poly` eyes (eyeball and iris) and a cornea as a separate glossy shell over each. Shown
  on the near level of detail. Brows and lashes are shown too.
- **Mouth:** the pack's teeth (`teeth_base`) and tongue (`tongue01`), seen when the mouth opens.
- **Expressions:** MPFB's expression units, baked as shape keys and exported as morph targets on the head:

| Expression | Made of | When the game shows it |
|---|---|---|
| `blink` | both eye closures | every 2 to 6 seconds, for 0.12 s, differently for each player |
| `effort` | mouth compression, brows down, nose compression, neck strain | gathering and in the air for a finish, a fade, a step-back; reaching for a steal |
| `shout` | mouth open, brows up, mouth retraction | celebrating |
| `dejected` | inner brows up, mouth depression | dejected |

  Each comes in and goes out over 0.15 s. They are driven by `src/render/face.js` from what the renderer already knows.
- The teeth, tongue and eyes stay put when the mouth opens: the shapes move the face round them.

## 7. Stage 3: hair and gear

### 7.1 Hair, modelled by script

`tools/blender/hair.py` builds each style as geometry on the scalp of the player it is for, weighted to his head:

| Style | How it is made |
|---|---|
| `buzz` | a shell 2 mm off the scalp; a picture of short hair over skin |
| `fade` | a shell from 1 mm over the ears to 6 mm at the crown, its picture fading from skin to full hair; a curly top 2.5 cm tall |
| `curls` | a shell 4 to 7 cm high, flat on top (a high top), pushed out in curl-sized clumps (about 1.2 cm) |
| `afro` | the pack's `afro01`, or a round shell 8 cm out, clumped as `curls` |
| `twists` | 60 to 90 short two-strand twists, tubes 6 mm thick and 4 to 8 cm long, standing up and out from the scalp |
| `locs` | 40 to 60 tubes 7 mm thick and 18 to 30 cm long, hanging; their lower parts weighted to two spring bones (`hairLocsLeft`, `hairLocsRight`, children of the head) |
| `cornrows` | 6 to 8 rows from the hairline back, each a chain of overlapping braid lobes 8 mm wide, with scalp between them |
| `short` | the pack's `short02` and `short04`, as now |

Hair has its own material: rough 0.5, a sheen, a normal map of strands generated by `maps.py`; colour from
`hairColour`. The brows stay the pack's.

### 7.2 Gear

Each built from the surface of the body it sits on, as the kit is, with 2 to 3 mm of thickness:

- **Headband:** a band 4 cm tall round the forehead, above the brows.
- **Arm sleeve:** from the shoulder to the wrist on one arm.
- **Leg sleeve:** from just under the shorts to the ankle on one leg, or a knee-length one.
- **Wristband:** 6 cm at one or both wrists.
- **Shoes:** high-top basketball shoes, made from the pack's `shoes05`: the collar raised 6 cm above the ankle, a
  thicker sole with a separate rim, a heel counter and laces as a normal map; painted at load in white with the
  team's colour. Socks show above them.

Gear is coloured at load: white, black, or the team's colour, as `people.json` says. The skin under a sleeve is
removed, as under the kit.

### 7.3 Who wears what

`people.json` gains `hairStyle` (one of 7.1) and `gear` (a list: `headband`, `armSleeveLeft`, `armSleeveRight`,
`legSleeveLeft`, `legSleeveRight`, `wristbandLeft`, `wristbandRight`, each with a colour). The 16 players are
given varied, invented looks: every style used at least once, about half of them wearing something.

## 8. Stage 4: skin and sweat

### 8.1 Skin

`MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial` for skin, on its own:

- **Colour:** the MakeHuman skin picture, as now.
- **Roughness that varies:** a map generated per skin from the body's regions: 0.45 on the forehead, nose and upper
  lip, 0.55 on the cheeks, 0.6 on the body; never under 0.4, so it does not look glossy.
- **Pores:** a 256-pixel tiling normal map of pores generated by `maps.py`, repeated about 40 times across the face's
  picture and fading out with distance from the camera.
- **Light through skin:** this Three.js has no subsurface skin material, so it is approximated: a reddish sheen
  (colour about #4a1e14, 0.35) that warms the edges and the shadow side. On Low and Medium, none.
- **Shadows:** the key light's normal bias is raised to 0.02 so that skin does not show shadow acne.

### 8.2 Sweat

- **How much:** each player has a sweat level from 0 to 1, kept by the renderer (the match never reads it). On court
  it rises with effort, by how fast his energy falls, and slowly with time (full after about 10 minutes of hard
  play); on the bench it dries over 5 minutes. A new match starts dry.
- **Where:** a sweat mask per skin, generated from the body's regions: the forehead, temples, upper lip, neck, top of
  the chest, shoulders and arms.
- **What it does:** where the mask is, roughness falls by up to 55% and the colour darkens by up to 8%; on Ultra, a
  tiling normal map of droplets is added. The jersey darkens by up to 12% at the middle of the chest and the upper
  back.

## 9. Stage 5: levels of detail and speed

- **Two levels of detail per player:** near (section 6: about 30,000 to 45,000 triangles with hair and gear) and far
  (about 15,000, as now, with the hair and gear simplified and no eyes, teeth or tongue). Both are bound to the same
  skeleton; the renderer shows one.
- **By quality:** Ultra and High show the near one within 9 m of the camera and the far one beyond it; Medium and Low
  always show the far one, with `MeshStandardMaterial` in place of the physical ones, no pores, droplets or sheen.
- **Draw calls:** at most five per player (skin, kit, hair and gear, eyes and mouth, and the far one's single mesh).
- **Pictures:** WebP in the file. Per player: one colour picture (2048 pixels on Ultra and High, 1024 on Medium, 512 on
  Low) and one normal map (1024, near level only). Shared by all: the fabric, pore, strand and droplet tiles.
- **Materials are shared** between players wherever only a picture or a colour differs.
- **File size:** `assets/players.glb` at most 35 MB (13 MB now); the single file about 50 to 60 MB (36 MB now).
- **Frame rate:** measured on this computer before and after each stage, as the time a frame takes to draw with ten
  players on court, at every quality level. Low may cost at most 10% more than now; High at most 30% more. A stage
  that passes those is cut back before the next begins.

## 10. Testing

- **The file:** each player has both levels of detail, the 52 bones plus the new ones by name, the four expressions as
  morph targets, the materials of each stage; the triangle counts within section 9; no skin triangle under the kit,
  sleeves or shoes (by position against the garments' surfaces).
- **Code:** the spring bones (they hang still at rest, swing and settle when the player stops, stay within 25 degrees,
  stop when paused), the expressions (blink timing, effort during a finish, shout on a celebration), sweat (rises on
  court, dries on the bench, starts dry), the choice of level of detail by distance and quality.
- **Everything that is there now still passes**, including the motions, the planted feet and the ball in the hands.
- **By eye:** the contact sheets and stills of each stage (section 4).

## 11. Not in this

- Strand-by-strand hair, true subsurface scattering, cloth simulated while playing.
- Scanned faces, tattoos, facial hair beyond the skins' own, women players.
- KTX2 texture compression, which needs an encoder downloaded: only if the Chromebook runs out of memory, and asked
  for first.
- Changing the motions to suit the new kit.

## 12. What could go wrong

- **The animations on new meshes.** Thicker, looser kit and new hair are weighted from the skin; at extreme poses (the
  windmill, the stretch of a layup) cloth can pass through a leg or an arm. The springs are limited to 25 degrees for
  this; the motions' film strips are checked with the new kit on.
- **The cloth simulation in the build.** It can tangle or take long. If it does, the garments are draped by a simpler
  shrink-and-push pass instead, and the folds come from the wrinkle map alone.
- **Generated hair up close** will look modelled, not real; twists and locs have the best chance, fades the worst.
- **The Chromebook.** It is not measured. Low and Medium keep today's cost; the stills and frame rates are from this
  computer.
- **Morph targets on joined meshes.** The game today joins every part of a player into one mesh; the head with its
  expressions must stay a mesh of its own.
- **Size and load time.** The file roughly doubles in size, so the first load from Drive is slower.

## 13. As built (2026-11-15)

Built in the five stages, each looked at in film strips, contact sheets (`docs/superpowers/stills/2026-11-15-models/`:
`before`, `stage1`, `stage2`, `stage4`, `after`) and close-ups in the game. Where it differs from the sections above:

- **Kit (5).** The jersey is tucked into the shorts (a loose hem ballooned into a skirt). Bindings are strips sewn
  along the edges. The picture wraps round the body and each leg (laid on flat, the sides were squeezed into a
  sliver). From the chest up the jersey takes the weights of the skin under it, and its looseness tapers to 2% at the
  shoulders. Two spring bones (the shorts' legs), not four; the locs add two more. High has no cloth sheen (it cost
  27% a frame); Ultra has it.
- **Body and face (6).** The skin keeps the face, ears, neck, hands, shoulders and knees whole and 55% of the rest: 87-90%
  of it in all (the plan said 75% overall; with skin taken away under hair, gear and cloth, an overall share starved
  the rest and streaked the neck). Skin the kit covers well is taken away. The cornea is the front of the high-poly eyes'
  own shell, black and additive. Teeth and tongue are one mesh.
- **Hair (7.1).** Grown from each head's own skin above a hairline. Twists droop, locs lie on the head and hang, the high
  top is short at the sides. The afro stays the pack's: a modelled one read as a mushroom, its hairline running over
  the ears. Each style has its own small picture; there is no fade mask.
- **Gear (7.2).** As written, but the shoes are painted in the build (it knows each player's team), and the high-tops
  keep the pack's sole (a thicker one would lift him off his planted feet): no separate rim, heel counter or laces map.
- **Skin and sweat (8).** The masks are the skin's vertex colours, not a picture. Sweat follows the match's own clock.
- **Levels of detail (9).** The far model is made in the build: each player copied, without eyes, mouth or
  expressions, simplified to 15,000 triangles. Each mesh drawn costs about 27 microseconds on this computer whatever
  its size, which is why Low and Medium draw only the far one. The near model draws ten meshes, not five; it is drawn
  only within 9 m on High and Ultra. Pictures are WebP.
- **Budgets met:** near 29,000-38,000 triangles (≤ 45,000), far 15,000 (≤ 18,000); `players.glb` 34.5 MB (≤ 35); the
  single file 64.5 MB (the spec guessed 50 to 60; the limit is 80). Frame time on the same seeded match against the branch point, back to back: Low 108%,
  Medium 91%, High 111% (budgets 110% and 130%). Ultra was 143% before the far models and not measured since.
- **Not checked:** the Chromebook; Ultra up close in the game (the test pane could not capture it); skin through cloth
  is checked by eye only. Known: a few white flecks of binding at the jersey's straps up close.

## 14. Early-2000s kit (after play-testing, 2026-11-15)

The owner asked for the look of early-2000s basketball kit instead of a modern fitted one, with a round neck, and
showed two photographs of the era for reference (looked at only; nothing of them is in the game or the repo).

- **Cut:** the jersey 17% loose at the chest (9% before), still tucked but bloused over the waistband, its arm holes
  14 cm under the shoulder joint; the shorts reach 6 cm below the knee (3 cm above before) and flare to 55% at the
  hem, the shin bones now part of their cut. The cloth is 6 mm thick and draped heavier, with broader folds; bindings
  2.5 cm at the collar, 4.5 cm at the waistband, 3 cm at the hems.
- **Collar:** a round neck on a smooth curve (11.5 cm deep at the front, 3.5 at the back), not cut from the skin's own
  coarse surface: the cloth along it is subdivided smoothly and trimmed to the curve, held still in the drape, and its
  binding smoothed along the loop. Round the neck the jersey follows the spine and neck (a quarter of the shoulders,
  none of the arms), so the collar keeps its round shape when he leans or swings his arms: it no longer sags into a U.
- **Paint:** the team's name (WAVES, SCORPIONS) arched across the chest over a big number, a long name lettered smaller
  to fit; name and numbers white, outlined in the darker team colour and raised; white piping down the side panels;
  wide darker stripes down the shorts, piped in white.
- **Fabric:** glossy: roughness 0.42 for the jersey's open mesh (a coarser knit), 0.38 for the satin shorts.
- **Budgets:** near 35,000-42,000 triangles, far 13,000 (from 15,000, to keep the file at 34.5 MB). Frame times were
  not re-measured after this change.

## 15. Kit as it is worn today (after the owner's review, 2026-11-17)

The owner found the jerseys did not look right, showed two photographs of today's league for reference (looked at
only; nothing of them is in the game or the repo), and chose: a modern cut, matte cloth with a soft sheen, the trim,
the chest lettering, the shorts' trim and the socks redone, and the city on the road colours with the nickname on the
home whites. It reverses section 14's cut and fabric.

- **Cut (tools/blender/kit.py):** the jersey 8% loose at the chest (17%), 6% at the hem (10%), still tucked; the
  shorts 10% loose at the hips (16%), flaring to 30% at the hem (55%), ending 4 cm above the knee joint (6 cm below).
  The cloth is 3 mm thick (6 mm) and draped lighter and softer.
- **Bindings:** each strip now faces out, as the cloth does, whichever way round its edge loop was walked; one walked
  the other way faced in and was thickened down into the cloth, under its surface, so the cloth showed through it in
  patches (the "torn" collar and arm holes). It stands 4 mm off the cloth along both its edges (1.5 to 3 mm before),
  after its smoothing every point of it is lifted back to that height wherever the cloth has come nearer, and it
  reaches 0.5 mm past the cloth's edge (2 mm), so that a hem's binding does not hang below it. Cloth over the
  bindings' outward faces: 1-4% of a jersey's (13-23% before), up to 7% of the shorts', the rest where a binding's
  edge just touches the cloth, at the bottom of the arm holes, on the straps and where a loop meets itself; a test
  holds it at 8% or less.
- **Paint (src/render/kit.js), at run time:** the chest word bigger, on a stronger arch across most of the chest;
  piping in a colour that stands out from both the panels and the cloth; the shorts' side stripes nearly twice as
  wide, with a trim line round each leg above the hem; white or black crew socks (repainted over the sock's corner of
  the shoes' picture); and no clear pixels next to a binding in any garment picture (they frayed the far model).
- **Fabric:** matte, roughness 0.8 for the jersey and 0.74 for the shorts, with a soft sheen on Ultra.
- **Left as they were:** a small notch where each hem's binding meets itself near the inseam, and a little flap of the
  jersey at the bottom of the arm hole (both there before).
